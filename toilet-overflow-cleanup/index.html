Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Lehigh Acres

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

Our Lehigh Acres crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. Clean water means extraction and drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the WaterTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Insurance

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • Supply lines that give out or tanks that crack, releasing clean water across the floor.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • A stuck flapper or fill valve that keeps the tank running without stopping.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Subfloor Saturation

Water gets beneath tile and vinyl through grout lines and the toilet base. Plywood below swells and delaminates, which can release the flooring glued to it.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Lingering Odor

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.
