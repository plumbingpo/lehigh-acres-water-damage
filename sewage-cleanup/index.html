Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Lehigh Acres, Lehigh Acres Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
